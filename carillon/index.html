    How to play

    Read. Tap. Listen.

    1. 1

      Read

      Every dot has a reach, drawn as a faint circle. Hold a dot to sweep it out and see which dots its ring would pop.

    2. 2

      Tap

      Lift your finger on the dot to tap it. It pops, its ring grows, and every dot the ring touches pops and sends out its own.

    3. 3

      Listen

      Every pop is a note, so the chain plays a tune. Pop every dot within the taps the level allows to clear it.

    What makes it special

    Simple rules, a field to read

    • One tap, a whole chain

      Tap a dot and it pops, sending out a ring. Every dot the ring reaches pops in turn and sends out a ring of its own.

    • Every pop is a note

      A dot's colour is its note on a pentatonic scale and its size is the octave, so every chain plays a little melody.

    • Hold to read, for free

      Hold a dot to sweep out exactly how far its ring reaches and which dots it would pop. Holding never costs a tap.

    • A spare tap on every level

      Each level allows one tap more than its goal, the fewest taps that clear it. Spend it learning the field, or keep it for three stars.

    • A hint from the solver

      The hint lights the best next tap from wherever the field is now, and shows what its ring will pop.

    • A new rule every chapter

      Each chapter of 20 levels brings its own idea to the field: heavy bells, tuning forks, horns and more.

    About the game

    Behind Carillon

    The name

    A carillon is a set of bells, usually in a tower, played from a keyboard. The name fits: every pop rings a note, and a full clear sounds like a short song.

    Chain reactions are a joy to watch, but most chain-reaction games are a race: tap fast and hope. Carillon slows the idea down. Rings are exact circles that grow at one speed, and holding a dot shows exactly what its ring will pop, so the puzzle is reading the field, never guessing.

    How it is made

    Every level is grown by a generator and then proven by a solver that finds the fewest taps that clear it. That number sets the taps a level allows and how the stars are scored. The levels are ordered so each one is at least as hard as the one before, and no two are the same, even turned or mirrored. The tests check all of that again on every build.

    A dot's colour is its note in the C major pentatonic scale, C, D, E, G and A, and its size is the octave. A pentatonic scale has no intervals that clash, so a chain in any order still sounds like music. Every note is a marimba tone synthesised in code, and so is every other sound. The game is written in GDScript with the Godot Engine, and the same code runs on Android and in your browser.

    How it stays free

    Carillon is free to play, in your browser and in the Android app. The web version never shows ads and sells nothing.

    The Android app has a few ads, placed so they never touch a puzzle: a banner below the field and on the level list, an ad between levels now and then, and short videos you can choose to watch, for a hint or to try a pack. Its shop is optional, since every level opens by playing. The Supporter pack turns the ads off for good and makes every hint free, and the other packs, level keys and the tip jar help pay for the next game.

    Questions

    Frequently asked

    How do I play Carillon?

    Tap a dot to pop it. Its ring grows out to the dot's reach, and every dot the ring touches pops and sends out a ring of its own. Clear every dot within the taps the level allows. Hold a dot first to see what its ring would reach. Lifting your finger on the dot taps it, and sliding off cancels.

    Is Carillon free?

    Yes. Carillon is free to play in your web browser, with no ads and nothing to buy. The Android app is free too. It shows a few ads, never during a level, and its shop is optional: every level opens by playing.

    Does Carillon need an internet connection?

    No. Carillon plays completely offline, and your progress is saved on your device. The Android app goes online only for its ads and for purchases through Google Play. Without a connection no ads appear, and a hint that would ask for a video is given anyway.

    How do stars work, and what is the spare tap?

    Every level has a goal: the fewest taps that clear it, found by a solver. A level allows one tap more than its goal. Clear it within the goal for three stars, or with the spare tap for two. Run out of taps with dots left and you can retry, or take one more tap once a try, which earns one star if it clears the field.
