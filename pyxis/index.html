    Pyxis

    Pull the knots of a tangled web apart. When no strand crosses another, the web comes clear.

    Play nowPlay it earlyPlay on the web

    A calm untangling puzzle

    Every level is a web of knots and straight strands, and some of the strands cross. Drag a round knot anywhere and let it go. When no strand crosses another, no knot sits on a strand and no two knots crowd each other, the web turns green.

    • Free to play
    • No timers
    • Plays offline
    • Never an ad mid-level

    How to play

    Look. Pull. Clear.

    1. 1Look

      Rust marks every tangle: two strands that cross, a knot sitting on a strand, or two knots too close together.

    2. 2Pull

      Drag any round knot anywhere and let it go. Strands may cross on the way, and only letting go counts as a move.

    3. 3Clear

      When nothing is tangled, light runs along every strand and the web turns green. Clear it within the goal's number of moves for three stars.

    What makes it special

    Small rules, tangled webs

    • Pull a knot, let it go

      Drag any round knot anywhere. Strands may cross on the way, so a drag never fails, and one good pull can clear a whole web.

    • Square knots stay put

      A square knot is an anchor. It never moves, so the rest of the web has to make room around it.

    • Rails and rings

      Some knots slide along a dashed rail and some follow a dashed circle, and later chapters turn and shrink the tracks.

    • A hint that shows the way

      The hint rings the knot to move and circles a place it can go. It never moves anything for you.

    • Stars for the short way

      Every web has a goal, the moves in a route that clears it. Match it or beat it for three stars. Undo never counts.

    • Real constellations

      Some levels are real constellations, played on the night sky. Each opens on its figure, then tumbles into a tangle, and once you clear it the stars glide home, from little Aries to Orion the hunter.

    About the game

    Behind Pyxis

    The name

    Pyxis is a small, faint constellation of the southern sky. Nicolas-Louis de Lacaille introduced it in the 1750s as a mariner's compass, and its brightest stars run in a short, almost straight line, like a compass needle. The word itself is Greek and Latin for a small box.

    A cleared web is a few points joined by straight lines with nothing crossing, which is how a constellation looks on a star chart. So many cleared webs look like one that the game took a constellation's name, some of its webs are real constellations, and its Constellation look draws every knot as a star.

    Why a game about webs is named after a constellation

    How it is made

    Every web is built from a solved layout. Each chapter draws its own kind: scattered webs, wheels around a fixed hub, tidy lattices, lines that cross at every knot, spirals, twin clusters, and real constellations projected from the sky the way a star chart draws them. Square knots keep their place in the solved layout and every rail and ring passes through it, while the other knots are tangled in the chapter's own way, so moving each knot back to its spot always clears the web.

    A measure of what each web asks of you, from the moves its goal takes and the fewest knots any solution has to move to how many crossings there are and how far the pulls reach, rises from one level to the next, so none is easier than the one before. No two webs are copies of each other, turned, mirrored or relabelled. The art and the sounds are made in code, and the music, Room to Breathe, has a melody that rises as the web comes clear.

    How it stays free

    Pyxis is free to play, with no account, no timers and no energy to wait for.

    It has a few ads, placed so they never touch a puzzle: a banner below the web and at the foot of the title, the level list and each result, an ad between levels now and then, and short videos you can choose to watch, for a hint or to try a pack. Its shop is optional, since every level opens by playing. The Supporter pack turns the ads off for good and makes every hint free, and the other packs, level keys and the tip jar help pay for the next game.

    Questions

    Frequently asked

    More answers, and a place to report a bug
    How do I play Pyxis?

    Drag a round knot anywhere and let it go. Each level is a web of knots joined by straight strands, and it is clear when no strand crosses another, no knot sits on a strand and no two knots crowd each other. Square knots never move, rail knots slide along a dashed line, and ring knots follow a dashed circle.

    Is Pyxis free?

    Yes. Pyxis is a free Android game, coming soon to Google Play. It shows a few ads, never during a level, and its shop is optional: every level opens by playing.

    Does Pyxis need an internet connection?

    No. Pyxis plays completely offline, and your progress is saved on your device. The Android app goes online only for its ads and for purchases through Google Play. Without a connection no ads appear, and a hint that would ask for a video is given anyway.

    How do I get three stars?

    Every web has a goal: the number of moves in a route that clears it. Clear the web in that many moves or fewer for three stars, in a few more for two, and in any number for one. The goal is a route the game found, not always the shortest, so you can sometimes beat it. Undo takes back a move without counting it, and restart begins the web again.
