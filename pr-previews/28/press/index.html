    About the studio

    Chaosmos makes games for phones, from calm logic puzzles to a platform adventure sewn from cloth. Shravan Goswami makes them one at a time. Each game starts with a simple control and gives it more to do as you play.

    The games are free to start and have optional purchases. Ads stay outside active play. The Android games can be played offline.

    The name is chaos and cosmos in one word. A puzzle starts as a scatter of pieces and ends in an order the player found.

    Tilt the maze and every marble rolls. Get each ball into the hole of its colour.

    Longer description

    Tilt the maze and every marble rolls at once. Get each ball into the hole of its own colour, and keep them all out of the pits.

    Plumb is a calm logic puzzle about gravity. Swipe to tilt the board and every marble rolls until a wall, the edge or another marble stops it. A marble drops into a hole of its own colour the moment it rolls over one. Square pits swallow any marble, so plan the route before you tip.

    One gesture moves everything, which is what makes four directions surprisingly deep: every move is a small chain of consequences to read before you make it.

    Tap a dot to start a chain reaction and pop the field. Every pop is a note.

    Longer description

    Tap one dot and start a chain reaction that pops the whole field. Every pop is a note, so every chain plays a little melody.

    Carillon is a calm chain reaction puzzle. Tap a glowing dot and it pops, sending out a ring. Every dot the ring touches pops in turn and sends out its own. Clear the whole field within the taps a level allows. A dot's colour is its note and its size its octave, so a full clear sounds like a song.

    The levels are not a race. Nothing moves until you tap, so each level is a quiet reading puzzle: which one tap reaches the most, and which dots can only be reached by a tap of their own.

    Key features

    • Three stars for the fewest taps, with one spare tap when you need it
    • A hint that lights the best tap and outlines everything its chain reaches

    • No timers on the levels, no lives, no energy
    • A soft dawn palette with the night sky a tap away, and a chiming pentatonic sound, with sound and vibration you can switch off

    Tap arrows to escape the orbit and clear the sky. A calm space logic puzzle.

    Longer description

    Tap an arrow and it flies straight out of orbit. Clear every arrow from the sky without a miss, in a calm arrow escape puzzle set around a glowing sun.

    Apsis is a logic puzzle set in a small planetary system. Arrows rest on faint orbits around the sun, each one pointing some way. Tap an arrow and it flies straight the way it points. If nothing is in its path it leaves the system for good. If another arrow or the sun is in the way, it springs back and you lose a heart. Clear every arrow to finish the level.

    The circle is what makes it different: straight paths cut across curved orbits, so which arrow is in whose way has to be read, not counted along a row.

    Key features

    • Every level built backwards from a solution, and none is easier than the one before
    • Three stars for a run without a miss, and three hearts before a retry
    • A hint that lights an arrow with a clear way out and draws its path

    Drag the knots until no line crosses another. A calm untangle puzzle and brain teaser, where a cleared web often settles into a real constellation.

    Every level is a web of knots and strands, and some strands cross. Drag a round knot anywhere and let go. When no strand crosses another and no knot sits on a strand, the web is clear. The name comes from the sky too: Pyxis, the little compass in the southern sky.

    Square knots never move, so you work around them. Some knots slide along a rail, others follow a circle, and every chapter changes how a pull unfolds.

    • Real constellations to untangle on the night sky, from little Aries to Orion the hunter

    Seiche is a calm puzzle about a pond full of fish. A seiche is a slow wave that rocks a whole lake from shore to shore, and here every tap sends one across the water.

    Tap the water and every fish in line with the ripple swims straight away from it. A fish keeps swimming until the bank, a rock or another fish stops it, or until it swims into a net. Get every fish into the nets. One tap can send fish two ways at once, or push a whole line of them together, so the trick is choosing where the ripple starts.

    Short description

    Slide wooden panels along their grooves to open the box and find the treasure.

    Longer description

    Hako is a calm puzzle about opening wooden puzzle boxes.

    Each box is a face of sliding panels. A panel moves only along its groove, and only where nothing stands in its way. Slide the panels aside until the black lid can leave through the gap, and the box is open.

    Later boxes bring brass pins. A pinned panel stays put until the panel with its mark rests on the matching ring, so some boxes open only in the right order, like the secret boxes of Hakone.

    The last box of every chapter holds a treasure for your shelf: a copper coin, a paper crane, a brass bell, and rarer finds the deeper you go.

    Key features

    Short description

    Hold anywhere to swing your planets and smash what flies at your star.

    Longer description

    You are a small star, and your planets do the fighting.

    Hold anywhere on the screen. How far your thumb is from the star sets how wide your planets swing, and they smash whatever they touch. Pull in to block what comes fast. Swing wide to reach what keeps its distance. That is the whole control.

    Each stage sends rocks, comets, pulsars, swarms and boulders at your star, and ends once the sky is clear. Smashed hazards drop light, and light buys upgrades: more planets, moons, a second ring that turns the other way, a pulse from the star, burning trails and more. Pick a few each stage and find what works together.

    Every stage is its own place, from a comet festival at sunrise to a whirlpool by starlight, and every chapter ends on a boss with a name of its own.

    After level 3, Endless opens: a sky that never clears, with bosses, health kits and fruit. How long can you keep the star burning?

    Key features

    • One thumb, one rule, learned in seconds
    • Stages that grow steadily harder, chapter by chapter
    • A new hazard or twist each chapter, met first on its own
    • A boss at the end of every chapter
    • Twelve upgrades that stack in different ways every time
    • Planets, trails, stars and smash bursts to collect: watermelons, disco balls, fireballs and more
    • Endless, with bosses, health kits, fruit and its own leaderboard
    • A daily sky, the same for everyone, against the clock
    • Every win makes a card to share, save or replay
    • Day and night looks and colour themes
    • Sound, music and vibration you can turn down or off
    • Plays offline

    A platform adventure in a world sewn from cloth, where one tap turns the pull of gravity.

    Longer description

    Guide a young runner through rooms of linen, felt and thread. Collect every button, then slip out through the zipper. Tap to turn gravity a quarter turn, so a wall becomes the floor and the ceiling becomes the way across. Every room plays one trick on you. Watch for the tell and try again.

    Each world pulls differently: water softens a fall, dune wind pushes, canopy air lifts, the night quilt stretches a jump and frost keeps you sliding. A Wardrobe lets you choose runners, trails, unravel styles, buttons and fabrics. Stitchfall began as an entry to GitHub's Game Off 2026.
